The Moon crosses the whole zodiac in about 27⅓ days. Vedic astrology divides that path into **27 Nakshatras**, or lunar mansions, each covering **13°20′** of the sky: roughly one for every day of the Moon's journey. This page lists all 27 with the details astrologers use most, then explains what each column means.

If you're new to the idea, start with [what a Nakshatra is](/blog/what-is-a-nakshatra); this page is the reference you'll come back to.

<KeyTakeaway>
The 27 Nakshatras run in a fixed order from <strong>Ashwini</strong> (0° Aries) to <strong>Revati</strong> (end of Pisces). Each has a ruling planet from the Vimshottari sequence, a presiding deity, a symbol and four padas of 3°20′. The Nakshatra your Moon occupied at birth is your <strong>Janma Nakshatra</strong>.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Reading the columns

### Span

The span is measured in the **sidereal** zodiac that Vedic astrology uses. If you compare against a Western (tropical) chart, positions will be roughly 24° further along; see [Rashi vs Sun sign](/blog/rashi-vs-sun-sign). Nine Nakshatras cross from one sign into the next, which is why the span of Krittika, for example, starts in Aries and ends in Taurus. For how the signs and Nakshatras interlock, see [Rashi vs Nakshatra](/blog/rashi-vs-nakshatra).

### Ruling planet

Notice the pattern: **Ketu, Venus, Sun, Moon, Mars, Rahu, Jupiter, Saturn, Mercury**, repeated three times. That sequence is the order of the [Vimshottari Dasha](/blog/vimshottari-dasha-guide). The lord of the Nakshatra your Moon sat in at birth is the planet whose period your life begins with, and how far the Moon had travelled through that Nakshatra decides how much of the period remained.

### Deity

Each Nakshatra has a presiding deity drawn from the Vedic pantheon: Agni (fire) for Krittika, Rudra for Ardra, Vishnu for Shravana and so on. Astrologers use the deity to describe the Nakshatra's traditional character. Ardra, ruled by the storm-god Rudra, is associated with upheaval and renewal, for instance. Treat these as symbolic descriptions from the tradition rather than predictions.

### Symbol

The symbols are old mnemonic images, often tied to the shape of the star group. Several come in pairs: the front and back legs of a bed for the two Phalgunis, and the front and back of a cot for the two Bhadrapadas.

## The three Ganas

Nakshatras are also grouped by temperament, or **Gana**. The grouping is used mainly in [Guna Milan](/blog/guna-milan-ashtakoota-compatibility) compatibility matching.

| Gana | Nakshatras |
| --- | --- |
| **Deva** (divine) | Ashwini, Mrigashira, Punarvasu, Pushya, Hasta, Swati, Anuradha, Shravana, Revati |
| **Manushya** (human) | Bharani, Rohini, Ardra, Purva Phalguni, Uttara Phalguni, Purva Ashadha, Uttara Ashadha, Purva Bhadrapada, Uttara Bhadrapada |
| **Rakshasa** (fierce) | Krittika, Ashlesha, Magha, Chitra, Vishakha, Jyeshtha, Mula, Dhanishta, Shatabhisha |

"Rakshasa" can sound alarming, but it describes an intense, independent temperament in the tradition, not a bad person or a bad chart.

## The stars behind the names

Nakshatras are named after real star groups, and several are easy to find in the night sky:

| Nakshatra | Commonly identified star(s) |
| --- | --- |
| Krittika | The Pleiades |
| Rohini | Aldebaran |
| Ardra | Betelgeuse |
| Punarvasu | Castor and Pollux |
| Magha | Regulus |
| Chitra | Spica |
| Swati | Arcturus |
| Jyeshtha | Antares |
| Shravana | Altair |

The zodiac divisions are equal 13°20′ segments, so a Nakshatra's span and its namesake star don't always coincide exactly. The segment is what astrology uses.

## Padas and naming syllables

Each Nakshatra divides into four **padas** of 3°20′. Each pada carries a traditional starting syllable used in naming. A child with the Moon in Ashwini's four padas, for example, would traditionally be given a name beginning with Chu, Che, Cho or La. Families that follow this custom usually ask for the pada, not just the Nakshatra.
