For many Indian families, a new vehicle isn't driven anywhere important until it has been blessed. **Vahan puja** (*vahana* means vehicle) is that blessing: a short puja asking for safe journeys, performed when a car, scooter, bike or commercial vehicle first comes home.

<KeyTakeaway>
A vahan puja usually takes <strong>15 to 45 minutes</strong>. The vehicle is cleaned, <strong>Ganesha is worshipped first</strong>, the vehicle is marked with <strong>kumkum and a garland</strong>, a <strong>coconut is broken</strong>, <strong>aarti</strong> is performed, and in many regions <strong>lemons are crushed under the wheels</strong> on the first drive. You can do it at home, at a temple or even at the showroom.
</KeyTakeaway>

## When families perform it

- **On delivery day**, before the first proper drive.
- **On an auspicious day** from the [Panchang](/blog/what-is-panchang), if the family prefers to wait for one.
- **At festivals.** Vehicles are traditionally worshipped along with tools and instruments during **Ayudha Puja**, on the ninth day of Navratri, especially in South India; during **Vishwakarma Puja** in eastern India; and on **Dussehra** in many regions.

Many people also time the purchase itself for days considered auspicious for buying, such as Dhanteras or Akshaya Tritiya.

## Where to do it

| Place | What it's like |
| --- | --- |
| **Temple** | Very common. Many temples have a spot where priests bless vehicles daily. |
| **At home** | A simple family puja in the driveway or parking area. |
| **Showroom** | Some dealers arrange a quick puja at delivery. |

## What you'll need

- Kumkum, haldi and rice (akshata)
- A marigold garland and loose flowers
- A coconut
- Camphor or a small lamp, and matches
- Incense sticks
- Red thread (mauli / kalava)
- Four lemons, if your family follows that custom
- Sweets or fruit for prasad
- A small image or idol of Ganesha, optional

See the [puja samagri guide](/blog/puja-samagri-list) for what each item traditionally signifies.

## Regional customs

Vahan puja is widely practised but not uniform. You'll see differences in:

- **Which symbols are drawn**, and where.
- **Whether lemons are used**, and whether green chillies are hung with a lemon on the vehicle afterwards, a common folk custom against the evil eye.
- **Which deity is invoked** beyond Ganesha. Some families pray to their family deity, others to Hanuman or Durga for protection on the road.
- **Commercial vehicles**, which are often decorated more elaborately and blessed again each year at Ayudha or Vishwakarma Puja.

If you're following a family custom, that's the one to use.

## Faith and the road

A vahan puja is a prayer for safe journeys, and for many families it's an important way of beginning. It sits alongside, not instead of, the ordinary protections: seatbelts and helmets, sober and rested driving, and a well-maintained vehicle.
