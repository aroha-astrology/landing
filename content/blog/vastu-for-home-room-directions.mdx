Most people meet Vastu Shastra the same way: someone looks at a floor plan and says, *"the kitchen shouldn't be there."* Then comes the harder question — **so where should it be, and does it actually matter for my home?**

This guide answers the first part room by room, using the widely followed traditional directions. At the end, we show how to check your own home in a few minutes without holding a rulebook in your head.

<Callout>
Vastu is a traditional system, and traditions differ on the details. The directions below follow the rule set used in Aroha Vastu Studio (named **aroha-traditional-v1**) and note where other schools commonly differ. Treat them as guidance for how to use your space, not as predictions.
</Callout>

## The idea behind Vastu directions

Vastu divides a home into **eight directions** around a calm centre called the **Brahmasthan**. Each direction is associated with an element and a kind of energy:

- **North-East (Ishanya)** — water and clarity; kept the lightest, cleanest and most open.
- **East** — the rising sun; light, health and new beginnings.
- **South-East (Agneya)** — fire; the natural place for cooking.
- **South** — stability and rest.
- **South-West (Nairutya)** — earth; the heaviest, most grounded corner.
- **West** — gains and continuity.
- **North-West (Vayavya)** — air and movement; suits things that come and go.
- **North** — wealth and opportunity; kept open and uncluttered.

The **centre** is ideally left open — no heavy walls, pillars or a toilet at the heart of the home.

Everything that follows is simply this map applied room by room: rooms that need fire go where fire lives, rooms that need stillness go where the home is heaviest, and the purest corner is kept for prayer.

## Room-by-room Vastu directions

### Main entrance

- **Best:** North, North-East or East
- **Acceptable:** North-West
- **Avoid:** South, South-West, South-East and West

The entrance is where energy — and people — come in, so it is one of the most important placements in the home. A bright, clean, clutter-free entrance in the northern or eastern side is the traditional ideal.

### Puja room (prayer room)

- **Best:** North-East
- **Acceptable:** East or North
- **Avoid:** South, South-West and South-East

The North-East is the purest zone of the home. Even if you don't have a separate puja room, a small shelf or corner here, kept clean and uncluttered, follows the same principle.

### Kitchen

- **Best:** South-East
- **Acceptable:** North-West or East
- **Avoid:** North-East, South-West and North

Fire belongs in the fire corner. Many traditions also suggest the cook faces **east** while cooking — often easier to change than the room itself.

### Master bedroom

- **Best:** South-West
- **Acceptable:** South or West
- **Avoid:** North-East, South-East and North

The heaviest, most stable corner suits the head of the household. Sleeping with the head towards the **south** is the most common traditional recommendation.

### Second bedroom and guest room

- **Best:** North-West or West
- **Acceptable:** South or North
- **Avoid:** North-East and South-East

The airy North-West is traditionally linked with movement, which is why it suits guests — and, in many homes, grown children who will one day move out.

### Children's room

- **Best:** West or North-West
- **Acceptable:** North or East
- **Avoid:** South-West and South-East

For a study corner, facing **north or east** while studying is the usual advice.

### Living room

- **Best:** North or North-East
- **Acceptable:** East or North-West
- **Avoid:** South-West and South-East

A living room on the northern or eastern side stays lighter and more welcoming through the day.

### Dining area

- **Best:** West or East
- **Acceptable:** North or North-West
- **Avoid:** South and South-East

### Bathroom and toilet

- **Best:** North-West
- **Acceptable:** West or North
- **Avoid:** North-East, East, South-East and South-West

A toilet in the North-East is one of the most commonly flagged Vastu issues. If yours is there, don't panic — see the remedies below.

### Store room

- **Best:** South-West
- **Acceptable:** South, West or North-West
- **Avoid:** North-East and East

Heavy storage belongs in the heavy corner; keep the North-East light.

### Staircase

- **Best:** South, South-West or West
- **Acceptable:** South-East
- **Avoid:** North-East, North and East

### Balcony

- **Best:** North, North-East or East
- **Acceptable:** North-West
- **Avoid:** South-West and South

Open spaces are traditionally placed where you want more light and air to come in: the north and east.

### Parking

- **Best:** North-West or South-East
- **Acceptable:** West or South
- **Avoid:** North-East and East

### Water storage

- **Best:** North-East or North
- **Acceptable:** East or North-West
- **Avoid:** South-East, South and South-West

<Callout>
Schools differ here: the North-East placement is usually given for **underground** water (a sump or borewell). Many traditions place an **overhead** tank in the South-West instead, because it is heavy. If you are planning construction, check which kind of tank the guidance you're following refers to.
</Callout>

## If a room is in the "wrong" place

Very few homes follow every rule — especially apartments, where you rarely choose the layout. Traditional guidance is gentle about this. The usual order of steps is:

1. **Use the room differently where you can.** A spare room in the North-East can become a study or prayer corner; a heavy cupboard can move to the South-West.
2. **Keep problem areas clean, dry, bright and uncluttered.** This is the most common advice for a North-East toilet or a cramped entrance.
3. **Small, non-structural remedies.** Many traditions suggest things like a bowl of sea salt in a bathroom, a tulsi plant in the North-East, or good lighting in a dark corner.
4. **Structural changes only when you renovate.** Moving a kitchen or bathroom is a big decision — worth considering when you are rebuilding anyway, not as an emergency.

<Callout tone="caution">
Vastu is a traditional framework for arranging space, not a guarantee of outcomes. Don't make costly or unsafe structural changes on the strength of a direction chart alone.
</Callout>
