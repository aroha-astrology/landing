A new shop shutter going up, the first day in a new office, a factory's machines switched on for the first time: across India, beginnings like these are often marked with a puja. It's a way of asking for the venture's success and of gathering the people who'll make it work.

<KeyTakeaway>
Business openings most often include a <strong>Ganesh puja</strong> (for an unobstructed start) and a <strong>Lakshmi puja</strong> (for prosperity), sometimes with a <strong>Vastu Shanti</strong> for new premises or a <strong>Satyanarayan Katha</strong>. Many trading communities also worship new account books (<strong>Chopda Pujan</strong>) at Diwali, and workshops honour tools and machines at <strong>Vishwakarma</strong> or <strong>Ayudha Puja</strong>.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Which puja, for which beginning

| Occasion | Pujas commonly performed |
| --- | --- |
| **Opening a shop** | Ganesh puja, Lakshmi puja; puja of the cash counter and first stock |
| **Opening an office** | Ganesh puja, Lakshmi puja; sometimes Saraswati puja; Satyanarayan Katha |
| **New premises, bought or built** | [Vastu Shanti](/blog/vastu-shanti-puja), often with a havan |
| **Factory or workshop** | Ganesh puja; Vishwakarma Puja; puja of machines and tools |
| **New account books** | Chopda Pujan / Sharda Pujan at Diwali |
| **New company vehicle** | [Vahan puja](/blog/vahan-puja-new-vehicle) |

## Why Ganesha and Lakshmi

**Ganesha** is worshipped at the start of any undertaking as the remover of obstacles; almost every puja begins with him. **Lakshmi** is the goddess of prosperity and fortune, so she's central to anything involving commerce. Many businesses also honour **Saraswati**, goddess of knowledge, particularly where books, learning or creative work are involved, and some traders worship **Kubera**, the guardian of wealth, alongside Lakshmi.

## Choosing a date

Most families consult a priest or the [Panchang](/blog/what-is-panchang) for a muhurat. Some days are widely regarded as good for beginnings:

- **Akshaya Tritiya**, traditionally considered auspicious for starting ventures and for purchases.
- **Dhanteras** and **Diwali**, especially for trading businesses.
- **Vijayadashami (Dussehra)**, a traditional day for new beginnings.
- **Regional new years**: Gudi Padwa, Ugadi, Puthandu, Vishu, Baisakhi and others.

Many businesses simply choose a good muhurat close to their practical opening date.

## Chopda Pujan: worshipping the account books

In Gujarati, Marwari and many other trading communities, Diwali marks the start of the traditional business year. New account books, the *chopda* or *bahi khata*, are worshipped with kumkum, flowers and a swastika or other auspicious marks on the first page, and the first entries are made at a chosen muhurat. Many businesses now do the same with a new ledger, a laptop or simply the year's first invoice. On Diwali, Indian stock exchanges hold a short **Muhurat Trading** session in the evening, a modern echo of the same custom.

## Vishwakarma and Ayudha Puja: tools and machines

**Vishwakarma**, the divine architect and craftsman, is honoured in workshops, factories and garages, especially in eastern India, where Vishwakarma Puja falls in September. In South India, **Ayudha Puja** on the ninth day of Navratri honours tools, machines, instruments and vehicles; work pauses for the day and everything is cleaned and decorated.

The last point matters in shared workplaces. A puja can be a warm, inclusive start when colleagues are invited rather than expected, and when everyone is welcome to share the sweets.
