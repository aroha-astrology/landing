Moving into a new home is one of the bigger moments in a family's life. In Hindu tradition it's marked with **Griha Pravesh**, literally "entering the home": a ceremony held before, or at the start of, living in a new house or flat. It asks for blessings on the home and the people who will live in it.

<KeyTakeaway>
Griha Pravesh is performed at an <strong>auspicious time (muhurat)</strong> chosen from the Panchang. A typical ceremony includes <strong>Ganesh puja</strong>, <strong>Kalash sthapana</strong>, the family's <strong>ceremonial first entry</strong>, a <strong>Vastu puja</strong> and often a <strong>havan</strong>, and the custom of <strong>boiling milk until it overflows</strong> in the new kitchen. Details vary by region and family.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Three kinds of Griha Pravesh

Traditional texts and priests commonly distinguish three situations:

| Type | When |
| --- | --- |
| **Apoorva** | Entering a newly built or newly bought home for the first time |
| **Sapoorva** | Returning to a home after a long absence, such as a long stay abroad |
| **Dwandwah** | Re-entering after major renovation or repairs, or after a disaster |

The ceremony for an Apoorva Griha Pravesh is usually the fullest; the others are often simpler.

## Choosing a muhurat

Most families fix the date with a priest or astrologer using the [Panchang](/blog/what-is-panchang). Traditions differ, but factors commonly considered include:

- **The month.** Magha, Phalguna, Vaishakha and Jyeshtha are frequently cited as favourable. Adhik Maas (the intercalary month) and Kharmas (when the Sun is in Sagittarius or Pisces) are commonly avoided, and some families avoid the Chaturmas period.
- **The Tithi.** Many avoid the "Rikta" Tithis (the 4th, 9th and 14th) and Amavasya.
- **The Nakshatra.** Nakshatras such as Rohini, Mrigashira, Chitra, Anuradha, Uttara Phalguni, Uttara Ashadha, Uttara Bhadrapada and Revati are often cited as favourable.
- **The time of day.** The chosen time avoids Rahu Kaal and aligns with a favourable ascendant (lagna) where the priest calculates one.

Treat these as the tradition's guidance. A priest from your own community may use somewhat different rules, and many families balance the ideal date with the practical realities of possession dates and work schedules.

## Preparation timeline

| When | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| **3–4 weeks before** | Fix the muhurat; book the pandit; decide the scale of the ceremony and guest list |
| **2 weeks before** | Confirm whether the pandit brings the samagri or you arrange it; get the list |
| **1 week before** | Finish repairs and deep cleaning; make sure water, gas and electricity are working |
| **2–3 days before** | Buy or confirm samagri, flowers and food; arrange seating and a safe space for the havan |
| **The day before** | Hang the toran, prepare the kalash items, set out the new vessel for milk |
| **On the day** | Rangoli at the entrance, family ready before the muhurat, phones on silent |

## What you'll need

The pandit will give a precise list for your tradition. A typical Griha Pravesh needs a kalash, coconut, mango leaves, a toran, flowers and garlands, kumkum, haldi, rice, betel leaves and nuts, ghee, a lamp, camphor, incense, fruits and sweets, a new vessel and milk, and, for a havan, a havan kund, wood and havan samagri. Our [puja samagri guide](/blog/puja-samagri-list) explains what these items traditionally signify.

## In a flat

Griha Pravesh in an apartment works well with a little planning:

- **Check with your society** about havan smoke, the lift and parking for guests.
- **Mind the smoke detectors.** A havan in a small flat can set off alarms; a smaller havan kund, good ventilation, or holding the havan on a balcony helps.
- **Keep fire safety in mind:** a metal tray under the havan kund, a bucket of water nearby, and nothing flammable overhead.
- **Tell your neighbours.** It's courteous, and the mantras and bells can carry.

For what to check before you move in, see [Vastu for flats and apartments](/blog/vastu-for-flats-apartments).

## Why families perform it

For many families Griha Pravesh is an act of faith: asking for the home to be blessed and protected. For others it's also about **marking a beginning**: gathering family, sharing food and turning a property into a home. Both are good reasons. How elaborate the ceremony is, and how strictly the muhurat is followed, is a family decision.
