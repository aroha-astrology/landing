If you're new to Vedic astrology, almost every explanation eventually points back to one document: your **Kundli**. It's the starting point for everything else — Lagna, houses, planetary strength, yogas, dashas — so it's worth understanding what it actually is before diving into the rest.

## What is a Janam Kundli?

A Janam Kundli, or birth chart, is a map of exactly where the Sun, Moon and every other planet were positioned at the precise moment you were born, viewed from your exact birthplace. It's calculated from three inputs:

- **Date of birth**
- **Exact time of birth**
- **Birthplace**

Change any one of these — especially the time — and the resulting chart can shift meaningfully, since houses and the Ascendant move quickly across the sky.

## What does a Kundli contain?

A full Kundli brings together several layers of information:

- **[Lagna](/blog/what-is-lagna-ascendant)** — your Ascendant, the anchor point of the whole chart
- **Rashi** — your Moon sign
- **[Nakshatra](/blog/what-is-a-nakshatra)** — the lunar mansion your Moon falls in
- **[Planetary positions](/blog/navagraha-nine-planets-vedic-astrology)** — where each of the nine Grahas sits, by sign and degree
- **[Houses](/blog/12-houses-vedic-astrology)** — the 12 life areas each planet is placed in
- **Aspects** — the relationships planets form with each other across the chart
- **Yogas** — specific planetary combinations traditionally linked to particular outcomes
- **Dashas** — the timeline of planetary periods that unfold over your life
- **Divisional charts** — finer-grained charts (like the Navamsa) used to examine specific life areas in more depth

## The D1, or Rashi chart

Among the many divisional charts a full reading can involve, the **D1 (Rashi) chart** — the main birth chart — is generally treated as the primary chart, the one that shows the overall picture before any divisional chart is consulted for more specific questions.

## North Indian vs South Indian chart styles

The same underlying data can be drawn in two common visual formats. The **North Indian** style uses a fixed diamond layout where the houses stay in the same position and the signs rotate into place; the **South Indian** style fixes the signs in place and lets the houses rotate instead. Neither is more "correct" — they're simply two regional conventions for displaying identical chart data.

## What can a Kundli be used for?

A Kundli is the reference point for reading:

- **Personality** — through the Lagna and 1st house
- **Career** — through the 10th house and its lord
- **Marriage** — through the 7th house, Venus and Jupiter
- **Education** — through the 4th and 5th houses
- **Finances** — through the 2nd and 11th houses
- **Timing** — through Dashas and transits
- **Major life themes** — through the chart read as a whole, not any single placement
