Vedic astrology, or Jyotish, builds its entire predictive system around nine bodies known as the **Navagraha** — literally, the "nine seizers" or nine planetary influences. Two of them, Rahu and Ketu, aren't physical planets at all; they're the lunar nodes, the points where the Moon's orbit crosses the Sun's apparent path. Jyotish treats all nine as Grahas because all nine are understood to exert a gravitational-style pull on a person's life, not because of a literal astronomy claim.

## Surya — Sun

Vitality, authority, confidence and leadership. The Sun is also traditionally read as significator of the father and of one's core sense of self.

## Chandra — Moon

The mind, emotions, and the mother. Where the Sun represents your identity, the Moon represents your inner world — how you feel and react moment to moment.

## Mangal — Mars

Courage, energy, competition and decisive action. Mars is the planet of drive — the willingness to push forward and take on a fight.

## Budha — Mercury

Communication, intelligence, business acumen and analytical ability. Mercury governs how you think, speak and process information.

## Guru — Jupiter

Wisdom, knowledge, expansion and teachers. In classical Jyotish, Jupiter is generally regarded as the most benefic of the nine — associated with growth, higher learning and good fortune.

## Shukra — Venus

Relationships, beauty, luxury and creativity. Venus governs attraction, aesthetics and what you find pleasurable.

## Shani — Saturn

Discipline, responsibility, delays and perseverance. Saturn has a reputation as astrology's "hard teacher" — but its themes are really about structure, patience and long-term results earned through consistent effort.

## Rahu

Ambition, unconventional experiences and material desire. As a lunar node rather than a physical body, Rahu is traditionally read as an intensifier — amplifying whatever house and sign it occupies, often in ways that pull a person outside convention.

## Ketu

Detachment, spirituality and introspection. Where Rahu pulls toward worldly ambition, Ketu — its opposite node — pulls toward release, inward focus and matters beyond the material.

## Why all nine matter together

No single Graha is read in isolation. A chart's overall character comes from how these nine interact — their house placements, their strength, the aspects between them, and the periods (Dashas) they rule over the course of a life. Learning what each one governs is the first step toward reading your own chart as a whole rather than one placement at a time.
