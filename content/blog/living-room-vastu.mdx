The living room is the most public room in a home: where guests are received, where the family gathers and, in most flats, the first room you enter. Vastu treats it accordingly. The guidance is about **openness towards the north and east** and **weight towards the south and west**.

<KeyTakeaway>
Vastu traditionally places the living room in the <strong>north, east or north-east</strong>. Heavy seating goes on the <strong>south and west walls</strong> so people sit facing north or east; the <strong>TV goes in the south-east</strong>; the <strong>north-east corner stays light</strong>, and the centre of the room stays open.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Where the living room goes

| Living room in the | Traditional view |
| --- | --- |
| **North** | Favoured; associated with openness and prosperity |
| **East** | Favoured; morning light |
| **North-east** | Favoured, as long as it stays light and uncluttered |
| **North-west** | Common and acceptable; associated with movement and guests |
| **South-west** | Less favoured; traditionally reserved for the master bedroom |

## Furniture: heavy to the south-west, light to the north-east

The same principle that governs the whole home applies inside the room. Treat the living room as a small plan with its own eight directions:

| Item | Traditional placement |
| --- | --- |
| **Sofa and heavy seating** | Along the south or west walls |
| **Seating direction** | Seated people face north or east |
| **TV, speakers, electronics** | South-east part of the room |
| **Heavy cabinets, bookshelves** | South or west walls |
| **Plants, light decor** | North or east; north-east kept especially light |
| **Centre of the room** | Open, with only a low table if any |
| **Staircase** (in a duplex) | South, west or south-west, rising clockwise |

### The north-east corner

This is the corner most practitioners care about. Keep it clean, light and uncluttered: a plant, a window, a light-coloured wall. Popular guidance sometimes suggests a small water feature or aquarium in the north or north-east, which is consistent with Vastu's association of the north-east with water. It's an option, not a requirement.

### The centre

The middle of the room, like the centre of the home, is traditionally kept open. A heavy central coffee table, a pillar or a pile of furniture in the middle of the room goes against that idea. A rug and a low table are fine.

## Light, colour and atmosphere

- **Windows on the north and east** are favoured, for gentle daylight.
- **Light, warm colours**: whites, creams, soft yellows and light greens are the usual suggestions.
- **Lighting**: a bright, well-lit living room is preferred to a dim one.
- **Images and art**: popular Vastu advice favours calm, positive images over scenes of conflict or sorrow. That's as much about the mood of a room as about tradition.

## In a combined living and dining room

Most flats have one long living-dining space. A practical way to apply Vastu there:

- Put the **seating area towards the north or east** half, near the entrance.
- Put the **dining table towards the west** half. West is the traditional dining zone.
- Keep the **path from the entrance through the room clear**, so you're not walking around furniture to get in.

## Keeping it practical

Living room Vastu is one of the easiest areas to apply because it's mostly about furniture, and furniture moves. Try the sofa on a different wall, clear the north-east corner and see how the room feels. If it works for you, keep it. If it doesn't, the tradition itself is flexible: practitioners disagree on details, and no reading requires you to live in a room that doesn't suit your family.
