Hindu tradition doesn't treat a life as one continuous stretch. It marks it with **samskaras**, ceremonies at key thresholds that sanctify a stage of life: a baby's name, a child's first solid food, the beginning of learning, a wedding and, finally, the rites after death. Many families still observe several of them, each with its own puja.

<KeyTakeaway>
The samskaras are traditionally counted as <strong>sixteen</strong>, though classical texts differ. Today families most often observe the <strong>naming ceremony (Namkaran)</strong>, <strong>first food (Annaprashan)</strong>, <strong>first haircut (Mundan)</strong>, the <strong>start of learning</strong> and the <strong>wedding</strong>, along with milestone birthdays such as <strong>Shashtipurti</strong> at sixty. Customs vary widely by region and community.
</KeyTakeaway>

## The sixteen samskaras

The *Grihya Sutras*, the classical manuals for household rites, describe the samskaras. Different texts give different lists; the one below is commonly cited.

| # | Samskara | Stage | What it marks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Garbhadhana | Before conception | Prayer for a child |
| 2 | Pumsavana | Pregnancy | Blessing for the growing baby |
| 3 | Simantonnayana | Pregnancy | Blessing the mother; today often a baby-shower ceremony |
| 4 | Jatakarma | Birth | Welcoming the newborn |
| 5 | Namakarana | Infancy | Naming |
| 6 | Nishkramana | Infancy | The baby's first outing, often to see the sun or a temple |
| 7 | Annaprashana | Infancy | First solid food |
| 8 | Chudakarana | Early childhood | First haircut (Mundan) |
| 9 | Karnavedha | Childhood | Ear piercing |
| 10 | Vidyarambha | Childhood | Beginning of learning |
| 11 | Upanayana | Childhood / youth | Sacred thread initiation, in communities that observe it |
| 12 | Vedarambha | Youth | Beginning of Vedic study |
| 13 | Keshanta | Youth | First shave |
| 14 | Samavartana | Young adulthood | Completion of studies |
| 15 | Vivaha | Adulthood | Marriage |
| 16 | Antyeshti | Death | Last rites |

Few families observe all sixteen today. Which ones are kept, and how, depends heavily on region, community and family tradition.

## The ceremonies families commonly observe

### Godh Bharai, Seemantham and baby showers

The pregnancy samskaras survive most visibly as celebrations for the expectant mother: **Godh Bharai** in much of north India, **Seemantham** or **Valaikaappu** in the south. Many include a small puja along with blessings, gifts and bangles for the mother.

### Namkaran: naming

The naming ceremony is traditionally held on the **eleventh or twelfth day** after birth in many communities, though timing varies. The first syllable of the name is often chosen from the pada of the baby's birth Nakshatra; our [list of the 27 Nakshatras](/blog/27-nakshatras-list) explains how padas and syllables work. A short puja is followed by the name being whispered in the baby's ear and announced to the family.

### Annaprashan: first solid food

Around **six months**, a baby is fed a first taste of rice, often kheer, after a puja. The ceremony has regional names: **Mukhe Bhaat** in Bengal, **Choroonu** in Kerala. Some families place objects such as a book, a pen and money before the baby to see which is picked up first, a playful folk custom.

### Mundan: first haircut

The first haircut is traditionally done in the **first or third year** (odd years are commonly preferred), often at a temple or with a puja at home. In many families the hair is offered at a temple.

### Karnavedha: ear piercing

Often combined with another ceremony or done on an auspicious day in early childhood.

### Vidyarambha: the start of learning

The child writes their first letters, traditionally in rice or sand, with a priest or elder guiding their hand. In Kerala and parts of South India it's celebrated widely as **Vidyarambham** on Vijayadashami; elsewhere it's called **Aksharabhyasam** or held at the start of schooling.

### Upanayana

The sacred thread ceremony is observed in some communities as the start of formal religious education. Its practice and meaning are specific to those communities.

### Vivaha: marriage

The wedding is the most elaborate of the samskaras, with a sequence of rites that differs enormously between regions. Many families consult a Kundli match first; see [Guna Milan](/blog/guna-milan-ashtakoota-compatibility).

## Milestone birthdays

Several later-life ceremonies, most common in South India, mark long life:

| Ceremony | Age | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Shashtipurti** (Shashtyabdapurti) | 60 | Completing one cycle of the traditional sixty-year calendar; often includes a renewal of the couple's wedding vows |
| **Bhimaratha Shanti** | 70 (77 in some traditions) | A later milestone of long life |
| **Sathabhishekam** (Sahasra Chandra Darshan) | Around 80 | Having seen a thousand full moons |

These are occasions for the whole family, often with a havan and a large gathering.

## After death

**Antyeshti**, the last rites, and the **shraddha** ceremonies for ancestors that follow, complete the cycle. They're deeply specific to each community and are almost always performed with a family priest.
