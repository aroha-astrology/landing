Most families don't need a priest for a daily puja. But for a [Griha Pravesh](/blog/griha-pravesh-puja), a [Vastu Shanti](/blog/vastu-shanti-puja), a Satyanarayan Katha, a naming ceremony or anything with a havan, they usually invite a **pandit** (also called a purohit or pujari) to the home. Finding the right one, and agreeing everything clearly beforehand, makes the day calmer.

<KeyTakeaway>
Before you confirm a pandit, agree six things: the <strong>tradition and language</strong> of the puja, the <strong>procedure and duration</strong>, the <strong>muhurat</strong>, <strong>who arranges the samagri</strong>, <strong>exactly what the fee covers</strong>, and <strong>what you need to prepare</strong>. Clear answers up front are the best sign of a good booking.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Where families find a pandit

- **The family priest.** Many families have a purohit who has served them for years or generations, often the best choice when available.
- **A local temple.** Temples often have priests who perform home ceremonies, or can recommend one.
- **Community associations.** Regional and community groups in most cities maintain contacts for priests of their tradition.
- **Referrals** from relatives, friends and neighbours.
- **Booking services** that match families with priests and handle scheduling.

## The questions to ask

| Ask | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| **Which tradition do you follow, and in which language will you explain?** | Procedures differ between regional and priestly traditions. Mantras will usually be in Sanskrit, but you'll want explanations in a language the family understands. |
| **What does the puja involve, and how long will it take?** | So you can plan guests, food and the day around it. |
| **Who will fix the muhurat?** | Some priests calculate it themselves from the [Panchang](/blog/what-is-panchang); others work to a time you give them. |
| **Will you bring the samagri, or should we arrange it?** | The single biggest source of last-minute stress. If you're arranging it, ask for the full list. |
| **What exactly does the fee cover?** | Dakshina, assistant priests, travel and samagri may or may not be included. |
| **Will there be a havan?** | In a flat you'll need to plan for smoke, ventilation and smoke detectors. |
| **What should we prepare?** | Space, seating, clothes, any fasting, items only the family can provide. |
| **When will you arrive?** | Early enough to set up before the muhurat. |
| **What if we need to reschedule?** | Especially important when the date depends on possession or travel. |

## Samagri: included, or arrange it yourself?

This choice shapes both the price and the preparation.

| | Samagri included | Arrange it yourself |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Convenience** | High: the pandit brings everything | You shop from a list |
| **Control** | Less say over brands and quality | Full control, e.g. natural kumkum, particular flowers |
| **Freshness** | Depends on the pandit | You buy flowers and fruit on the day |
| **Risk of missing items** | Low | Higher, unless the list is complete |
| **Price** | Higher quote, one payment | Lower quote, plus your purchases |

Neither is better; it depends on your time and preferences. What matters is that the choice is explicit. Our [puja samagri guide](/blog/puja-samagri-list) explains what the common items are for.

## What affects the cost

Prices vary a great deal, so it's more useful to understand what drives them than to rely on a single number:

- **The ceremony**: a simple Ganesh puja takes an hour; a Griha Pravesh with Vastu Shanti and havan can take half a day.
- **Havan or not**: fire rituals need more time and materials.
- **Number of priests**: larger ceremonies often need assistants.
- **Samagri included or not.**
- **City and travel distance.**
- **Season**: festival days and popular muhurats are in high demand.

Some priests leave **dakshina** to the family's discretion; many now quote a fixed amount. Either way, it's reasonable, and respectful to both sides, to agree the full amount in advance.

## On the day

- **Be ready before the muhurat**: space cleared, family dressed, phones silent.
- **Seat the pandit comfortably**, offer water, and have the samagri laid out if you arranged it.
- **Follow their guidance**; it's fine to ask what a step means.
- **Offer dakshina respectfully**, traditionally with both hands, often with fruit or a piece of cloth. Many families also serve the priest a meal.

## Warning signs

<Callout tone="caution">
Be cautious if a priest or service <strong>adds expensive extra pujas by warning of doshas or misfortune</strong>, won't give a clear price or samagri list in advance, or pressures you to decide on the spot. A good pandit makes the ceremony calmer, not more frightening.
</Callout>
