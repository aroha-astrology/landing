Before a Vedic wedding is arranged, it's common practice to compare the couple's birth charts using a system called Guna Milan — literally "matching of qualities." The version most widely used today, Ashtakoota ("eight kootas"), scores compatibility across eight specific factors, out of a maximum of 36 points.

## The eight kootas, and what each one checks

- **Varna** (1 point) — spiritual and temperament compatibility, based on each person's Moon sign group.
- **Vashya** (2 points) — mutual influence between the two, based on sign groupings.
- **Tara** (3 points) — general wellbeing, counted from each person's birth Nakshatra to the other's.
- **Yoni** (4 points) — physical compatibility, matched by the animal symbol assigned to each Nakshatra.
- **Graha Maitri** (5 points) — friendship between the planetary lords of each person's Moon sign.
- **Gana** (6 points) — temperament group (Deva, Manushya, or Rakshasa) of each Nakshatra.
- **Bhakoot** (7 points) — compatibility based on the distance between the two Moon signs, linked to prosperity and family wellbeing.
- **Nadi** (8 points) — health and progeny, based on each Nakshatra's Nadi (Vata, Pitta, Kapha) classification.

Notice the points aren't evenly weighted — Nadi alone accounts for over a fifth of the total 36, which is why it's treated as one of the two "critical" kootas.

## Why the total score isn't the whole story

A widely used rule of thumb treats 18 out of 36 as a workable minimum, with scores in the mid-20s and above considered strong — but that single number can be misleading. Nadi Dosha (both partners sharing the same Nadi, scoring zero on that koota) and Bhakoot Dosha (an unfavorable sign-distance between the two Moons) are traditionally weighted heavily enough that many astrologers check them independently of the overall total, even when the combined score looks fine.

## Cancellation (Bhanga) rules

As with other doshas in Jyotish, both Nadi Dosha and Bhakoot Dosha have traditional cancellation conditions — specific chart combinations under which the dosha is considered non-functional. Exactly which conditions count as cancellation varies between regional traditions, which is why the same two charts can occasionally get different verdicts from different astrologers.

## More than a scorecard

Ashtakoota is the most standardized layer of Vedic matching, but a fuller compatibility reading also looks at Manglik status (see [what is Manglik Dosha](/blog/manglik-dosha-explained)) and the broader strength of each chart. Guna Milan is a structured starting point, not a replacement for reading both charts properly.
