Search "astrology remedies" and you'll mostly find generic lists — wear this gem, chant this mantra, donate on this day. Classical Jyotish treats remedies (upay) as something matched to a specific chart, not a one-size-fits-all shopping list. Here's the actual landscape of traditional remedy categories, and where the generic-advice approach falls short.

## What Are Vedic Astrology Remedies?

**Upay**, commonly translated as remedies, are traditional practices — mantra, charity, fasting, worship, gemstones, yantras and others — associated in classical texts with easing the expression of a difficult planetary placement or supporting a favourable one.

## Why Are Remedies Recommended?

The underlying idea is that a planet's difficult placement in a chart isn't necessarily fixed and unchangeable — traditional practice offers ways to work with that placement rather than simply accept it passively.

## Mantra and Japa

Repeated chanting of a planet-specific mantra — **Japa** — is one of the most widely practiced remedies, generally tied to a specific count and a planet-appropriate mantra rather than a generic one.

## Charity and Donation

**Dāna**, giving associated with a specific planet's traditional correspondences (particular items, particular days), is a long-standing remedy category, often paired with fasting or worship on the same planet's associated day.

## Fasting and Vrat

Fasting on a day associated with a specific planet — Saturday for Saturn, Tuesday for Mars, and so on — is a traditional practice believed to support that planet's more favourable expression.

## Puja and Worship

Formal worship directed at a deity associated with a specific planet is a traditional remedy category of its own, distinct from mantra recitation, though the two are often practiced together.

## Gemstones

Specific gemstones are traditionally associated with specific planets — see [how Vedic astrology recommends gemstones](/blog/vedic-astrology-gemstones-guide) for how that Navaratna system works, and why verifying a stone's authenticity matters as much as choosing the right one.

## Yantras

Geometric diagrams — **yantras** — associated with specific planets or deities are another traditional remedy category, generally used alongside mantra or worship rather than as a standalone practice.

## Temple Visits

Visiting a temple associated with a planet's presiding deity, particularly on that planet's associated day, is a widely practiced remedy in many regional traditions.

## Behavioural and Ethical Remedies

Beyond ritual practices, classical texts also describe behavioural remedies — cultivating specific virtues or avoiding specific behaviours associated with a planet's more difficult expression — treated as complementary to, not a replacement for, the ritual categories above.

## How Remedies Are Traditionally Chosen

Selection is meant to start from the actual chart: which planet needs support, its house role, its dignity, and what's realistically appropriate for the individual — not a generic "top 10 remedies" list applied without reference to the chart at all.

## Why a Remedy Should Match the Chart

A remedy chosen for a planet that's already well-placed and strong in a chart isn't targeting the same need as the identical remedy chosen for a severely afflicted placement of the same planet in someone else's chart. Matching the remedy to the actual chart context is a core part of traditional practice, not an optional refinement.

## Can a Remedy "Cancel" a Planet Completely?

Traditional framing generally treats remedies as supporting or easing a placement, not erasing it outright. That's worth distinguishing from a genuine chart-based cancellation condition, like [Neecha Bhanga](/blog/neecha-bhanga-raja-yoga-explained), which works through the chart's own combinations rather than an external remedial practice. The two are related in spirit — both speak to a difficult placement not being the final word — but they're not the same mechanism.

## Common Misconceptions

- "One remedy fixes everything" — remedies are traditionally chart-specific and planet-specific, not universal fixes.
- "A more expensive gemstone is automatically more effective" — traditional selection criteria centre on the stone matching the right planet and being genuine, not on price.
- "Remedies replace effort" — classical framing generally treats remedies as support alongside effort, not a substitute for it.
