Vastu Shastra was written for houses on their own plots, with courtyards and open land around them. Most people today buy a flat: a set of rooms stacked among many others, with a fixed plan and a shared entrance. So how do you apply a tradition written for one to the other?

The practical answer is to **focus on your own flat's layout**, check the handful of features that are hard to change, and not lose a good home over details you can adjust after moving in.

<KeyTakeaway>
For a flat, check the <strong>entrance</strong>, the <strong>kitchen's zone</strong>, the <strong>master bedroom's zone</strong>, where the <strong>toilets</strong> are, and whether the <strong>centre</strong> is open. Facing matters less than people think, and floor number isn't part of traditional Vastu. Almost everything else can be adjusted after you move in.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Start with the plan, not the brochure

Before visiting, ask for the flat's **floor plan with a north arrow**. With it you can do most of the Vastu evaluation at your desk:

1. Confirm north (the brochure's arrow isn't always exact; check on site with a compass; see [how to find directions](/blog/vastu-directions-explained)).
2. Lay a **3 × 3 grid** over the flat's own plan to find its eight zones and centre. Our guide on [reading a floor plan with Vastu](/blog/how-to-read-floor-plan-vastu) shows how.
3. Note which room falls in which zone.

## The five things worth checking

These are the features that are genuinely hard to change once you've bought.

### 1. The entrance

Traditional preference is for the flat's own door to open in the **north, east or north-east**, with its position along the wall also mattering. The building's main gate matters less to most practitioners. See [main entrance Vastu](/blog/main-entrance-vastu).

### 2. The kitchen

The kitchen traditionally belongs in the **south-east**, with the **north-west** as the accepted alternative. A kitchen in the north-east or at the centre of the flat is the most commonly discouraged arrangement. Check where the gas line and platform are; see [kitchen Vastu](/blog/kitchen-vastu).

### 3. The master bedroom

Ideally in the **south-west**. If the largest bedroom is elsewhere, the bed can still be placed in that room's south-west part; see [bedroom Vastu](/blog/bedroom-vastu).

### 4. The toilets

This is one of the few areas where practitioners are fairly consistent: toilets in the **north-east** or at the **centre** of the home are discouraged. North-west and west are the usual preferred positions.

### 5. The centre

Is the middle of the flat open (a passage, a living area), or is it a toilet, a staircase or a heavy store? An open centre is preferred.

## What matters less than you might think

- **The building's facing.** For a flat, your own entrance and rooms carry more weight.
- **Floor number.** Traditional Vastu doesn't address floors in a tower. Beliefs about "lucky" floors are modern.
- **A slightly cut or extended corner.** Irregular plans are the norm in apartments. Practitioners differ on how much a missing corner matters, and it's rarely a reason to walk away.
- **Colours, mirrors and furniture.** All can be changed after you move in.

<Callout tone="caution">
Be wary of anyone who tells you a flat will bring illness or financial ruin because of its direction. That isn't what the classical texts say, and it's often a prelude to selling you an expensive "correction".
</Callout>

## After you move in

Once you've bought or rented, Vastu becomes about **use, not construction**:

- Choose which bedroom is the "master" based on zone, not just size.
- Place beds, the sofa and heavy wardrobes following the south-west-heavy, north-east-light principle.
- Keep the north-east corner and the centre clear.
- Set up the shrine in the north-east; see [pooja room Vastu](/blog/pooja-room-vastu).
- Many families mark the move with a [Griha Pravesh](/blog/griha-pravesh-puja), and some with a [Vastu Shanti puja](/blog/vastu-shanti-puja).

## When a flat doesn't "pass"

Almost no flat meets every guideline, and practitioners disagree on which ones are essential. If a home works for your family in every practical way (light, space, location, budget) and has a couple of Vastu imperfections, most sensible practitioners would tell you to buy it and arrange it well.
