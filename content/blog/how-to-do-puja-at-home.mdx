You don't need a priest, a special room or a long list of items to perform a puja at home. A lamp, a little water, a few flowers and a few quiet minutes are enough to begin. This guide walks through a simple puja step by step, then shows a shorter version for busy mornings.

Practices differ between regions and families. If your family has its own way, follow it; this is a general outline that most traditions would recognise.

<KeyTakeaway>
A simple home puja follows the logic of welcoming an honoured guest: <strong>prepare and purify</strong> the space, <strong>light the lamp</strong>, begin with <strong>Ganesha</strong>, make a short <strong>intention (sankalpa)</strong>, offer <strong>water, flowers and food</strong>, <strong>pray or chant</strong>, perform <strong>aarti</strong>, and close by sharing <strong>prasad</strong>.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Before you begin

- **Choose a time.** Morning after bathing is the most common; evening around sunset is the other. Some families also check the day's [Panchang](/blog/what-is-panchang) and avoid starting during Rahu Kaal.
- **Prepare yourself.** Bathe and wear clean clothes.
- **Prepare the space.** Clean the shrine or the spot you'll use. If you're setting one up, see [pooja room Vastu](/blog/pooja-room-vastu); traditional guidance is to face east (or north) while you pray.
- **Gather what you need** on a plate (thali) within reach.

## What you'll need

For a simple puja:

- A **diya** (lamp) with ghee or oil and a cotton wick, and matches
- **Incense** (agarbatti or dhoop), optional
- A small vessel of **clean water** and a spoon
- **Kumkum** (or roli), **haldi** (turmeric) and **chandan** (sandalwood paste), whatever your tradition uses
- **Akshata**: a few grains of unbroken rice, sometimes mixed with kumkum
- **Flowers**, and tulsi leaves if you worship Vishnu or Krishna
- **Naivedya**: fruit, sweets, sugar crystals or milk
- A **bell**, if you have one

Our [puja samagri guide](/blog/puja-samagri-list) explains what each item traditionally signifies.

## A five-minute daily puja

Most households don't do all ten steps every day. A short daily version might be:

| Step | Time |
| --- | --- |
| Light the lamp and incense | 1 minute |
| Offer water and a flower | 1 minute |
| Recite one prayer or mantra | 2 minutes |
| Wave the lamp (short aarti) and bow | 1 minute |

Keep the fuller version for weekends, festivals or a particular day of the week associated with your family's deity.

## What to recite

If you don't know any mantras, begin with a simple one for your chosen deity, or a well-known prayer such as the Gayatri mantra, or just speak from the heart. ## Practical tips

- **Fire safety first.** Keep the diya on a plate or stand, away from curtains and within sight, and never leave it burning unattended near children.
- **Ventilate.** Incense and camphor produce smoke; open a window, especially in small flats.
- **Fresh offerings.** Replace water and flowers daily; don't leave food out to spoil.
- **Used flowers.** Many families now compost them or bury them in a garden instead of putting them in rivers or lakes.
- **Be consistent rather than elaborate.** Most traditions value a regular, sincere practice over an occasional grand one.

## When to invite a pandit

Some ceremonies are traditionally led by a priest: a [Griha Pravesh](/blog/griha-pravesh-puja), [Vastu Shanti](/blog/vastu-shanti-puja), Satyanarayan Katha, life-event ceremonies and anything that includes a havan.
