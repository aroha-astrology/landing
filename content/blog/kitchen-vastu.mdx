If you remember one thing about kitchen Vastu, make it this: the kitchen belongs to **fire**, and Vastu gives fire the **south-east**. Almost every other kitchen recommendation follows from that and from one more idea: keep fire and water apart.

<KeyTakeaway>
Traditional Vastu places the <strong>kitchen in the south-east</strong> (north-west as the alternative), the <strong>stove in the kitchen's south-east</strong> so the cook <strong>faces east</strong>, and the <strong>sink towards the north-east</strong>, with space between stove and sink. Heavy storage goes on the south and west walls.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Where the kitchen goes

In Vastu's map of the five elements, the south-east is the zone of Agni, fire. That makes it the traditional home of the kitchen.

## Inside the kitchen

Vastu applies the same directional logic within the room itself. Think of the kitchen as a small plan with its own eight directions.

<FloorPlan highlight={['kitchen']} caption="An illustrative plan with the kitchen in the south-east. Inside it, the stove would sit in the kitchen's own south-east part." />

| Item | Traditional placement |
| --- | --- |
| **Stove / hob** | South-east part of the kitchen, so the cook faces east |
| **Sink and water filter** | North-east part of the kitchen |
| **Fridge** | South-west, south or west; avoid the north-east |
| **Microwave, oven, mixer** | South-east or south (they belong to the fire/electrical zone) |
| **Heavy storage, grain, utensils** | South and west walls |
| **Window or exhaust** | East, to let in morning light and let smoke out |
| **Dining table** (if in the kitchen) | West |

### Fire and water apart

The stove (fire) and the sink (water) are elemental opposites in Vastu. Traditional guidance is to keep them **apart**: not side by side on the same stretch of counter and not directly facing each other across the kitchen. On an L-shaped platform, putting the hob on one arm and the sink on the other usually solves it.

### Which way to face

Facing **east** while cooking is the most common instruction, with north as a second choice. In practice this means the hob sits against the east wall or in the south-east corner. An east-facing cook also gets the morning light, which is one of the places where tradition and practicality agree.

## Kitchen, door and bathroom

- **Not directly facing the main entrance**, in popular guidance, so the stove isn't the first thing seen from the door.
- **Not sharing a wall with a toilet**, or directly above or below one in a multi-storey home, where it can be avoided.
- **Not the passageway** through the home: a kitchen that people must walk through to reach other rooms is discouraged.

## Colours and upkeep

Warm colours are traditional for kitchens: shades of orange, yellow, terracotta or warm cream. Black and very dark finishes are commonly discouraged. Most practitioners put as much weight on **cleanliness** as on colour: a clean hob, no leaking taps, no stale food and a clutter-free counter.

## Where tradition meets practicality

A few kitchen rules sound mystical but have sensible effects:

- An **east window** gives morning light and cross-ventilation.
- **Separating the hob from the sink** reduces splashes near a flame and gives two people room to work.
- **Keeping the kitchen off the main circulation route** is good for safety.

None of this proves Vastu's claims about fire and prosperity. It does mean following these suggestions rarely costs you anything.

## In a modular kitchen

Most flat kitchens come with the platform, gas line and plumbing fixed. What you can usually still do:

<Checklist
  title="Kitchen changes that don't need a renovation"
  items={[
    'Place the hob or cooktop at the east or south-east end of the platform if the gas line allows',
    'Leave working space between the hob and the sink',
    'Move the fridge away from the north-east corner',
    'Keep heavier storage on the south and west side',
    'Keep the exhaust or window clear so the kitchen stays ventilated',
    'Keep counters clear and fix any dripping tap'
  ]}
/>

If your kitchen is in the north-east or another discouraged zone, the usual practitioner advice is to make these arrangements within the room and leave the structure as it is. Nothing in the traditional texts suggests alarm, and a gas line should only ever be moved by a qualified professional for safety reasons, not for Vastu.
