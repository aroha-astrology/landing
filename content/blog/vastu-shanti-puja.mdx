Every Vastu text begins with the site. The ground a home stands on is, in the tradition, presided over by the **Vastu Purusha** and the deities who are said to rest on him. **Vastu Shanti**, "peace for the dwelling", is the ceremony that honours them: a puja, usually with a havan, asking that the home and its household be in harmony.

<KeyTakeaway>
Vastu Shanti is a <strong>puja and havan offered to the Vastu Purusha</strong> and the deities of the site. Families commonly perform it <strong>when moving into a new home</strong> (often as part of Griha Pravesh), <strong>after major renovation</strong>, or when taking over an older house. It is a devotional ceremony, not a physical fix for a building.
</KeyTakeaway>

## The idea behind it

In the story at the root of Vastu Shastra, a vast being was pinned to the earth by the gods, who then settled on his body; he became the Vastu Purusha, and each part of a site became associated with a deity. The **Vastu Purusha Mandala**, the grid that underlies Vastu planning, maps those deities onto the plan. Our guide to [what Vastu Shastra is](/blog/what-is-vastu-shastra) explains this in more detail.

Building on a site, digging into it and altering it are, in this tradition, acts that should be accompanied by respect and offerings to those deities. Vastu Shanti is that offering.

## When families perform it

| Occasion | Why, traditionally |
| --- | --- |
| **Before moving into a new home** | To bless the site before living there; often combined with [Griha Pravesh](/blog/griha-pravesh-puja) |
| **After major renovation or rebuilding** | Because the site has been disturbed |
| **Moving into an older or long-vacant house** | To begin afresh in a home with a history |
| **When a home departs from Vastu ideals** | As a traditional propitiation where the layout can't practically be changed |
| **Periodically** | Some families repeat it at intervals, or on a significant anniversary |

It usually takes **two to four hours**, longer if combined with Griha Pravesh and other pujas.

## Vastu Shanti and Griha Pravesh compared

| | Vastu Shanti | Griha Pravesh |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Focus | The site and its deities | The family's first entry into the home |
| Core rites | Vastu mandala puja, Vastu homa | Ganesh puja, Kalash, ceremonial entry, milk boiling |
| When | New home, renovation, older house | Moving in |
| Performed together? | Often, as part of Griha Pravesh | Often includes a Vastu puja or full Vastu Shanti |

## What it can and can't do

Vastu Shanti is sometimes sold as a cure.

In tradition, Vastu Shanti propitiates the site, and many families find real meaning in blessing a home before living in it. What it **isn't** is a change to the building. It doesn't move a toilet out of the north-east or bring light into a dark room. Practical issues are best handled practically; see [common Vastu mistakes](/blog/common-vastu-mistakes) for what's worth acting on. And no ceremony should be sold on the promise that, without it, harm will come to your family.

## Preparing for it

- **Book the pandit** and confirm the procedure your tradition follows, how long it will take and whether they include a havan.
- **Ask who arranges the samagri.** A Vastu Shanti needs more than a simple puja: typically kalashas, coconuts, mango leaves, rice and coloured powders for the mandala, flowers, ghee, havan wood and samagri, navadhanya (nine grains), fruits and sweets. See our [samagri guide](/blog/puja-samagri-list).
- **Clear a space** large enough for the mandala and havan kund, ideally near the centre or in the north-east, as your pandit advises.
- **In a flat:** plan for smoke with ventilation or a smaller havan, check smoke detectors, and keep water nearby.
