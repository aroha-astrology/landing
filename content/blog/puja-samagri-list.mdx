*Samagri* simply means materials, the things you gather before a puja. A daily puja needs only a handful of them; a Griha Pravesh or a havan needs dozens. Each item has a traditional meaning, and knowing it makes the ritual more than a shopping list.

Practices vary by region, community and deity, so treat this as a general guide and follow your family's or pandit's list where it differs.

<KeyTakeaway>
A basic kit is a <strong>diya</strong>, <strong>incense</strong>, <strong>water</strong>, <strong>kumkum and haldi</strong>, <strong>akshata</strong> (whole rice), <strong>flowers</strong> and a <strong>food offering</strong>, on a <strong>thali</strong>. Larger ceremonies add a <strong>kalash</strong>, <strong>coconut</strong>, <strong>mango leaves</strong>, <strong>betel leaves and nuts</strong>, <strong>mauli</strong> thread, <strong>panchamrit</strong> and, for a havan, <strong>havan samagri</strong>.
</KeyTakeaway>

## The everyday essentials

| Item | What it is | Traditional significance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Diya** | A lamp of clay or brass with a cotton wick in ghee or oil | Light dispelling darkness; the flame is treated as a witness to the puja |
| **Agarbatti / dhoop** | Incense sticks or cones | Fragrance offered to the deity; one of the five basic offerings |
| **Water (jal)** | Clean water in a small vessel (*lota*) | Purification; offered for washing and sipping in the upacharas |
| **Kumkum / roli** | Red powder | Auspiciousness; applied to the deity and as a tilak |
| **Haldi** | Turmeric | Purity and auspiciousness |
| **Chandan** | Sandalwood paste | A cooling, fragrant offering (*gandha*) |
| **Akshata** | Whole, unbroken rice, often mixed with kumkum | Completeness and prosperity |
| **Flowers** | Fresh flowers and garlands | Beauty and devotion; certain flowers are associated with certain deities |
| **Naivedya** | Fruit, sweets, milk or cooked food | Food offered to the deity, then shared as prasad |
| **Bell (ghanti)** | A small hand bell | Rung to begin worship and during aarti |
| **Thali** | A plate, often brass or steel | Holds the items; used for aarti |
| **Camphor (karpur)** | White camphor tablets | Burned during aarti; it leaves no residue, often read as a symbol of the ego dissolving |

## Items for larger pujas

| Item | Traditional significance |
| --- | --- |
| **Kalash** | A pot of water representing fullness and the presence of the divine; central to many ceremonies |
| **Mango leaves** | Placed at the kalash's mouth and in the door toran; auspicious and associated with new beginnings |
| **Coconut (shriphal)** | A pure offering; placed on the kalash or broken as an offering |
| **Betel leaves and nuts (paan, supari)** | Offered as *tambula*, a mark of respect; a supari is sometimes worshipped as Ganesha |
| **Mauli / kalava** | Red and yellow sacred thread tied on the wrist and around the kalash, for protection and as a sign of a vow |
| **Panchamrit** | A mix of milk, curd, ghee, honey and sugar, used to bathe images and shared as prasad |
| **Gangajal** | Water from the Ganga, used for purification in many homes |
| **Janeu** | Sacred thread offered to the deity in some pujas |
| **Cloth (vastra)** | A piece of new cloth, usually red or yellow, offered to the deity or used to cover the altar |
| **Tulsi leaves** | Offered to Vishnu and Krishna; traditionally not offered to Ganesha |
| **Bel (bilva) leaves** | Offered to Shiva |
| **Durva grass** | Offered to Ganesha |
| **Dakshina** | An offering of money, traditionally given to the priest |

## For a havan

A havan (fire ritual) adds its own set:

- **Havan kund**: a copper or iron container, often stepped, for the fire
- **Samidha**: dried sticks of wood, commonly mango
- **Havan samagri**: a prepared mix of herbs, grains, sugar and resins
- **Ghee**: offered into the fire with a spoon
- **Navadhanya**: nine grains associated with the nine planets, used in some pujas

For a [Griha Pravesh](/blog/griha-pravesh-puja) or [Vastu Shanti](/blog/vastu-shanti-puja), expect the larger-puja list above plus a toran, a new vessel for boiling milk, and havan items. ## Buying and storing

- **Buy consumables fresh**: flowers, fruit and betel leaves close to the day.
- **Check powders** for artificial colours; many families prefer natural kumkum and haldi.
- **Keep durable items together**, clean and dry, in the pooja space; see [pooja room Vastu](/blog/pooja-room-vastu).
- **Ask who supplies what.** For larger ceremonies, confirm whether the pandit brings the samagri or you arrange it; our guide on [booking a pandit](/blog/how-to-book-pandit-for-puja-at-home) covers this.

## Afterwards

Used flowers, leaves and rice are traditionally treated with respect rather than thrown in the bin. Many families now **compost them or bury them in a garden** rather than putting them in rivers and lakes, which avoids pollution while keeping the spirit of the custom. Ash from a havan is often scattered in a garden or plant pot.
